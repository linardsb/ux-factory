>       generate a phone call. And a complaint rate on the new journey above the current app average. Any one of those and we go back to the
>       phone for the affected segment.

*Question:* "What result would make us stop?" — Derived, from Duke's kill criteria and Shape Up's circuit breaker, which is the same idea implemented as a default · DERIVED (stage 7)
*Wrong if:* Pilot data crosses one of the named thresholds — elevated scam-loss rate, a cap-breaching single loss, sub-70% completion, or above-average complaints — and the affected segment is not rolled back to the phone journey
*Parent:* seq 8 (solution)
*Evidence:* none · ⚠ **no-evidence**

#### seq 16 · `s8-eval` — transition

> Two metrics, two owners, one cohort. Fraud operations own confirmed scam losses per thousand new payees, measured against the phone
>       baseline of the same months last year; pass is no worse. I own first-attempt completion, measured on the pilot cohort of 20,000
>       customers; pass is 85 per cent finishing without a call. The set is every new-payee attempt in the pilot, not a sample, and both are
>       read weekly in the same meeting.

*Question:* "What is the eval, who owns it, what is the pass rate, and against what set?" — Hamel Husain, Your AI Product Needs Evals (March 2024), best attested · OBSERVED (stage 8)
*Wrong if:* Scam-loss and completion figures are not in fact tracked against the whole pilot population of new-payee attempts, the 85% first-attempt completion or no-worse-than-phone-baseline thresholds are not what determines pass/fail, or the two metrics are not reviewed weekly by fraud operations and the journey owner together
*Parent:* seq 8 (solution)
*Evidence:* none · ⚠ **no-evidence**

#### seq 23 · `s6-edge-cases-or-refusals` — transition

> Scope: a close-match or no-match name result, because that is most sole traders; paying your own account at another bank; joint accounts; a first payment retried
>   because the account was short. Refusals we will say out loud: we will not send a payment to a new payee above the first-payment limit on day one; we will not let a
>   customer skip the name check; we will not offer it for international or business payments in this release; and we will not stop a customer paying after a no-match,
>   because it is their money and the rules do not let us, but we will not let them do it without having seen the result.

*Question:* "Which edge cases are scope, and which are refusals we will state out loud?" — Derived, Shape Up's no-gos applied to compliance · DERIVED (stage 6)
*Wrong if:* The release ships without a first-payment limit, allows a customer to skip the Confirmation of Payee check, blocks a customer from paying after a no-match result, or quietly extends to international or business payments
*Parent:* seq 7 (solution)
*Evidence:* none · ⚠ **no-evidence**

#### seq 26 · `s9-strength-of-evidence` — transition

> For the cost and demand: strong. Twelve months of contact-centre data, 38,000 calls a month, call listening on 200 of them, app-store reviews and twelve customer
>   interviews. For whether an app can replace the agent on scams: weak. We have the phone team's 300 refusals and industry reports, and nothing of our own. The
>   consequence of being wrong on the first is a business case that misses; on the second it is a reimbursement bill and a regulator. The evidence is proportionate to
>   the first and not to the second, which is why the pilot is 20,000 customers and not everyone.

*Question:* "What strength of evidence do you have, and is it proportionate to the consequence of being wrong?" — Marty Cagan, the evidence spectrum (Discovery — Judgement, SVPG); "How do you know?" is commonly attributed to him in this compressed form but could not be found in his own writing · THIN (stage 9)
*Wrong if:* The pilot is expanded beyond the stated 20,000 customers before the app's own scam-catch performance is measured, or the case rests on the phone team's 300 refusals and industry reports rather than evidence gathered from the app journey itself
*Parent:* seq 8 (solution)
*Evidence:* seq 1 — fictional-scenario — contact centre call volume/cost data (answer a1) · seq 2 — fictional-scenario — app store reviews (answer a1) · seq 24 — fictional-scenario — call-listening sample (200 calls) (answer a20) · seq 25 — fictional-scenario — customer interviews (12) (answer a20)
