Bring your design system. Build a pattern with it.

Drop your design tokens and this page maps them onto the same contract the rest of the site runs on, then puts real components on the stage wearing your values. Answer eight questions about your product and it drafts a breadboard you can edit. The pattern that renders from all of it is being built next.

Runs now · in your browser Nothing is uploaded No model is called

The rules are readable, so read them: system/pack-import.mjs maps an export onto the token contract and is the same engine the command-line importer runs, system/derive.mjs derives a full palette from one colour, system/build-questions.mjs holds the eight questions and the ethics verdict, and system/breadboard.mjs holds every rule that turns your answers into a breadboard. Your file is read here in your browser. The shipped site is static and has no server, so there is nowhere for it to be uploaded to.

01

Your product

Seven questions about the behaviour you are designing for.

From Nir Eyal's Hooked model: the feeling that brings someone back, the smallest thing they do, what they get back, what they put in, how often they would really do it, and the two questions behind the Manipulation Matrix. Every one arrives already answered with a recommended default and the reasoning behind it, so you can accept and move. Both ethics gates run beside the questions and update as you answer.

These questions run in your browser. With JavaScript blocked they do not run, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

02

Your shaping

Three questions about how big this is allowed to be.

From Shape Up (Ryan Singer / Basecamp): how much time the work is worth, the rough shape the solution takes, and what you are declaring out of scope. Appetite is a budget rather than an estimate, so it caps the board below; the no-go removes a place from it outright.

These questions run in your browser. With JavaScript blocked they do not run, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

03

Your breadboard

Places, affordances, and the lines between them.

Shape Up's breadboard is the lowest-fidelity design tool there is: places are the things you can navigate to, affordances are the things the user can act on, and connection lines show how the affordances take the user from place to place. No layout, no pixels. This one is drafted from your ten answers, and then it is yours to change: rename anything, add and remove places and affordances, and re-point any connection.

The breadboard is drafted in your browser from your answers. With JavaScript blocked there is nothing to draft it from and nothing to edit.
