## The three that cost the most

**Q1 — the fictional today.** Every derived field in the pack (`status` on plants and tasks, which rows are in the Today list, the "3 DAYS OVERDUE" label) hangs off one date the pack refers to five times and never states. I had to reverse-engineer it from a sample record and a chip label.
The sentence that would have removed it: *"The scenario's fixed today is 2026-07-15; all `status` values and the Today list are derived against that date."*

**Q5/Q6 — what "Log care" does.** The pack names the button, names the intent, says the row `id` is "what the log-care commit sends", and stops. No verb, no path, no payload, no atomicity, and no word on whether logging water reschedules the next watering or moves `lastWatered`.
The sentence that would have removed it: *"Log care is `POST /api/care-log { taskIds }`; it marks each task done, sets `lastWatered`/`lastFertilized` to today, and schedules the next water task at `today + wateringIntervalDays`."*

**Q7 — `action` vs `type`.** The one place the pack is executable (the wrapper) disagrees with the one place it is normative (the contract). A backend serving the contract faithfully produces a row that renders "Undefined Fiddle-leaf fig", and nothing in the pack says which side is wrong.
The sentence that would have removed it: *"The CareTask field is `type`; the wrapper's `data` setter maps `type → action`."*
